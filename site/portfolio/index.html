case study

From a shared inbox to a system

Where I work, every request to add a supplier or change how one is paid arrives by email, in whatever shape the sender chooses. I had the idea to move that work into the ticketing system the company already owned, ServiceNow. I designed the requests and wrote the proposal. IT built it from my design. It is live today, and we are just starting to tell people about it.

Once people are using it, I plan to track three things: the share of requests that arrive through the portal, how many are sent back for something missing, and the time from request to close.

The mess

  • Requests come in many shapes, to one shared inbox.
  • Nothing is required, so documents go missing and are found late.
  • The person asking cannot see any status.
  • Approvals are forwarded by email, with no record on the request.
  • There are no numbers on volume or turnaround.

What I designed

  • Four request types that cover most of the team's daily requests.
  • Every field, and which ones are required.
  • The rules that stop an incomplete request at the door.
  • Who each request goes to, and which ones need a second department's approval.
  • What happens when a request is rejected.
  • The old steps beside the new ones, so IT could see exactly what changes.

New Vendor Setup

Cannot be sent without
The supplier's legal name, country, type, what they will do for us, and their tax form.
Who handles it
The vendor team reviews it. Procurement approves when approval is needed. The new vendor number is posted to the ticket.

Payment / Bank Change

Cannot be sent without
The vendor, the kind of change, and a signed form or an official bank document.
Who handles it
The vendor team checks the document, then confirms the bank details by phone on a number they find themselves.

Existing Vendor Update

Cannot be sent without
The vendor, the kind of update, and what is changing.
Who handles it
Straight to the vendor team. Before this, there was no form for it at all.

Payment Terms Change

Cannot be sent without
The vendor, the terms being asked for, and a written business reason.
Who handles it
Procurement approves first. Only then does the vendor team make the change.

how i work

Built with AI, checked by me

Everything on this page was built in collaboration with AI systems. I say so plainly because it is part of the skill.

I decided what the request types are, which fields are required, who approves what, and where the bank confirmation call fits. AI helped me draft the proposals, the forms, the guides and the help tool. I read and checked every page before it went anywhere.

Nobody asked me to do this. I saw a mess, and I wanted it to be better.

The way I go about it

  • Find the mess. Watch how the work really arrives and where it gets stuck.
  • Design the fix. Decide what must be asked, who decides, and what happens on a no.
  • Write it down. A proposal clear enough that someone else can build it.
  • Make it easy to follow. Guides, cheat sheets and tools for the people on both sides.
